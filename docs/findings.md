# Findings

Quran-Nazm starts from one observation: Uthman Taha's hand is beautiful, but the manual typesetting of the Madina Mushaf is uneven. This file records what was measured, what was decided, and why. The two HTML documents in this folder hold the full evidence with figures:

- `madina-variance-evidence.html` — chapters 1–3, measurements of the Madina 1441H print
- `hizb-layout-study.html` — hizb sizes, layout renders and the shrink-limit comparison

All measurements use the 1441H (current) King Fahd Complex edition.

## 1. Lines carry very different amounts of text

Every line of the 1441H print has the same width; the text on it does not. Each line's words were shaped once, at one size, with no stretching, in the QMH New Madina (DigitalKhatt) font, and compared with the print's line width.

| Measure (8,790 full-width lines) | Value |
|---|---|
| Words on a full line | 4 to 15 |
| Letters on a full line | 21 to 52 |
| Natural length vs. line width | 62% to 136% |
| Lines off by more than 10% | 33% |

Page 156 (Al-A'raf 44–51) is the most uneven full page: line 11 runs 25% long. On page 72, lines 10–12 all hold 9 words, yet run +11%, −22% and +14%: word length, not word count, drives the problem.

## 2. Pen weight and texture vary

Measured on the scan (720 × 1123 px pages, about 100 ppi) over 7,320 lines on 488 all-text pages:

- Pen width differs by 13% between a light and a heavy line (5th to 95th percentile), and typically 9% within one page.
- Page 102: line 5 is 16% heavier than line 4 directly above it.
- Page 561 is the heaviest page, 19% heavier than page 563.
- Weight follows crowding: stretched lines are written heavier, crowded lines lighter. Ink per letter spans 1.36× and correlates with crowding at −0.61.

At this resolution the numbers are relative between lines, not absolute widths.

## 3. Ayah markers cut off from their ayah

573 lines (6.5%) on 362 pages begin with the marker that closes the previous ayah. All 440 such lines on the all-text pages were confirmed on the scan. This is an accepted convention of the print, listed because a typesetter with more freedom can avoid it. Word spacing was examined but not reported: at 100 ppi, words in this hand overlap horizontally too often to measure gaps reliably.

## 4. Choosing the unit of consistency

Fixed page counts per unit, with one common line width, never shrinking:

| Unit → pages | Line width vs Madina | Median stretch | Worst stretch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rub' → 2 | 1.78× | 1.42× | 2.26× |
| Half hizb → 4 | 1.61× | 1.30× | 1.68× |
| Hizb → 8 | 1.51× | 1.22× | 1.42× |
| Juz → 20 | 1.14× | 1.15× | 1.22× |

Rub' lengths differ by 2.19×; hizb lengths by 1.35× (8.5 to 11.6 Madina pages, standard deviation 7.7%). The rub' target would need type at 56% of Madina size or extreme stretching, so it was dropped. **Decision: 10 pages per hizb** (Madina's own density, about 9 words per line). Juz 30 (hizbs 59–60), with its many short surahs, is a declared exception.

## 5. Layout method

Decisions, in the order they were made:

1. Every page ends at the end of an ayah.
2. Each hizb is set as one unstretched DigitalKhatt line, cut into 10 pages at ayah ends, then each page into 15 equal lines at word boundaries; an ayah marker never starts a line.
3. Refinement: all page cuts of a hizb are chosen together, and all line cuts of a page are chosen together. Cutting each at its nearest boundary independently left pages nearly empty beside long ayahs (worst line 3.40×) and lines up to half a word off target.

Effect on evenness (standard deviation of log stretch):

| | Madina 1441H | Nazm |
|---|---|---|
| Inside a page | 0.065 | **0.039** (−40%) |
| Neighbouring pages (median difference) | 0.051 | 0.046 |
| Between hizbs | n/a | 0.085 |

Line harmony on a page, what a reader sees, improves by 40%. What remains is the hizb-to-hizb length difference; it changes gradually and never shows within a spread.

## 6. Shrink limit

The shrink limit sets the type size and where stretch is centred, not the evenness. Hizbs 1–58 (analysis model):

| Limit | Type vs Madina | Median stretch | Worst stretch | Lines squeezed | Worst squeeze |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ≤10% | 75% | 1.34× | 1.94× | 0% | 4% |
| ≤15% | 79% | 1.26× | 1.83× | 1% | 9% |
| ≤20% | 84% | 1.19× | 1.73× | 3% | 14% |
| ≤25% | 90% | 1.12× | 1.62× | 8% | 20% |
| ≤35% | 103% | 0.97× | 1.40× | 41% | 30% |
| ≤45% | 122% | 0.82× | 1.19× | 89% | 41% |

"Squeezed" means the font's narrow forms and tighter spaces were not enough and the letters were scaled horizontally. Madina compresses 16% of its lines by more than 10%, always through the calligrapher's own forms. **Decision: 25%.**

## 7. The rendered Mushaf

`output/quran-nazm.pdf`, generated by `scripts/render_mushaf.py` (see `RENDERING.md`):

| | Value |
|---|---|
| Pages | 604 (580 for hizbs 1–58, 24 for juz 30), the same count as the Madina print |
| Type size | 93% of Madina 1441H |
| Line stretch | median 1.07×, 5th–95th percentile 0.89–1.29×, worst 1.79× |
| Lines compressed | 27% |
| Lines squeezed horizontally | 21%, at most 24% |
| Words whose ink touches a neighbour | 0 (checked on every line) |
| Line evenness inside a page | 0.039 (Madina 0.065) |
| Every page ends on an ayah | yes |
| No line starts with an ayah marker | yes |
| Juz 30 page density (average stretch) | 0.96× to 1.25× |
| Short surahs split across pages | 5 of 29 (86, 92, 96, 98, 111), each with at least 2 ayahs on each page |

The renderer starts every surah on a new line, which balances lines within surah segments and gives slightly larger type (93%) than the analysis model (90%), at the cost of more squeezed lines (21% vs 8%; see Collisions below).

**Juz 30.** Its two hizbs share exactly 24 pages. Because each short surah starts on a new line under a two-line header, a page there is judged by the lines it really needs at the common width rather than by text length. Three rules shape it: no page may need more than the 25% shrink limit; no page may be sparser than 1.35× average stretch; and a surah may only be split if at least two of its ayahs stay on each page. Within those rules, splitting a surah that would fit on one page is made far more costly than any imbalance. A first version without the sparseness rule kept 28 of 29 short surahs whole but left Al-A'la alone on a page at 1.52× stretch, which looked empty. With the rule, every juz 30 page lies between 0.96× and 1.25×, Al-A'la shares its page with the end of Al-Tariq at 1.06×, and five short surahs (86, 92, 96, 98, 111) span two pages. A 25th or 26th page does not avoid those splits.

**Collisions.** The first full render had words running into each other on compressed lines (page 18 was reported). The cause was the font's word-shrink features (`shr1`, `shrr`), which the quran-madina-html justifier applies word by word: shaped inside a whole line they shift a word into its neighbour (page 18: اذكروا into إسرائيل, إماما into للناس) or squash letters within it (واتخذوا, ءامنا). The renderer no longer uses them; a line that must compress tightens its spaces to at most 60% and is then scaled horizontally, which raised the share of horizontally squeezed lines from 14% to 21%. Every line is now checked for real ink contact between neighbouring words: candidate pairs come from intersecting glyph boxes and are confirmed by rasterising their outlines. Ten remaining contacts, almost all a small pause mark (ۚ ۗ ۖ) touching the tall stroke of a following كذلك or كمثل, are cleared by widening only that space; the report shows 0 collisions left.

**Surah headers** are drawn as vector art: a pale ribbon between two fine rules, a central cartouche with pointed ends and a double outline holding the surah name, a small diamond on each side, and an eight-pointed star at each end.

## 8. Open points

- **Horizontal squeeze.** At 25%, about one line in five is scaled horizontally, visibly on dense pages such as page 541 (Al-Mujadila 1–7). Two remedies: a lower limit (squeeze is rare at 20%), or compression forms in DigitalKhatt that are safe inside a full line (the existing `shr1`/`shrr` collide; see Collisions), so shrinking stays calligraphic.
- **Hizb 55** (from Al-Mujadila) is the densest hizb and dominates the compressed end; making it an exception would relieve it.
- **Juz 30 splits.** Five short surahs span two pages to keep every page evenly filled; ornamental continuation marks or a different header size could let more stay whole.
- **Scan resolution.** Pen-weight figures would sharpen with a 300 ppi scan.
